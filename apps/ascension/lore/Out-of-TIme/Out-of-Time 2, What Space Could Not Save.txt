# What Space Could Not Save

Before she was called Telekinesis, she was Reign, and before that she was simply a girl whose best friend could bend space.

Ella's secret came out the way most children's secrets do — carelessly, and to exactly the wrong person to keep it quiet, which in this case meant the right one. She could shorten a hallway into three steps. She could fold a locked door's distance down to nothing. The two of them used the gift the way children use anything they believe no one else has noticed: badly, joyfully, without weighing the cost of being caught somewhere they weren't supposed to be able to reach.

Then Ella found something underneath the first ability, the way a person sometimes finds a second, colder room behind a door they thought led outside.

She believed she could move through time as easily as she moved through space. She never tested it directly. Her fear wasn't for herself — it was for the people a changed yesterday might erase, and specifically, more than anyone else, for Reign. So she built a smaller kind of safety first: a bubble of folded space around her own body, sealed enough to isolate whatever happened inside it from whatever happened outside. Within that seal, she leapt.

Nothing outside the bubble so much as flickered. She tried again, this time with Reign held tight against her side, and the world outside stayed exactly as unbothered as before.

Neither girl had a name for what they'd built. Among people who would later study such things for a living, it would come to be called a Ghost Leap — a crossing made from inside an isolated fold, invisible to whatever watched the ordinary timeline for disturbances, because the disturbance never touched the timeline at all.

To the girls, it was only another secret, filed next to the others.

### What the bomb left behind

The attack that killed Ella's family found her and Reign somewhere else entirely, which meant Ella came home to a house that no longer had anyone in it to come home to. Grief did what grief does to a fifteen-year-old with an ability no one else on Earth understood: it gave her three years of purpose, spent chasing rumors, tracing movements, following one bad lead into a slightly less bad one, until the trail ended in front of the people who had planted the device.

She killed all of them.

It did not give her family back. It didn't even give her the quiet she'd expected. So Ella decided, with the same clear-eyed calm she'd used to plan the killings, that the only remaining option was to go back and stop the bombing from ever happening.

Reign said no. Changing history could break things neither of them would be able to see breaking until it was far too late to fix. Ella agreed with every word of that argument and did it anyway, because agreeing had never once been the same thing as being talked out of something for her.

She said goodbye like it was any other goodbye. Then she folded herself into the bubble and leapt.

She reached the morning of the bombing. She found the men assembling the device. She was a breath away from stopping them when the world around her simply stopped behaving like the world.

The Council's excavator arrived the way a hand arrives inside a fish tank — sudden, total, and from a direction the fish has no framework for noticing. It lifted Ella out of the timeline before she could finish what she came to do. The extraction left no new string behind it, no fracture for anyone to trace.

Almost none.

For a fraction of a second, something remained where Ella had been — a trace too small to register as anything but instrument noise to anyone not already looking for it. Then she was gone, and the bombing proceeded exactly as it always had.

### The waiting

Reign waited the way people wait for someone they refuse to grieve — for days, then months, then years that stopped announcing themselves as years and became simply the condition of her life. She never let herself believe Ella was dead, because dead would have been an ending, and nothing about Ella's disappearance had the shape of one.

So she investigated instead. She read everything the public record held about Ascended history and unexplained vanishings, cross-referenced incidents no official account could fully explain, and eventually found old security footage from the morning of the bombing — a girl on the edge of the frame who, at normal speed, appeared to simply stop existing between one frame and the next.

Slowed down, the footage told a different story. Someone had taken her.

That single fact reorganized everything Reign had assumed for years. Ella hadn't vanished. She'd been removed, and removal implied someone capable of doing the removing, and someone capable of that was, at minimum, findable.

### The warehouse

The trail led, eventually, to a chemical shipment, and the shipment led to a warehouse where people who had stopped being called by their names were being injected with substances that made some of them sick, some of them scream, and a small remainder something else entirely.

Reign watched from the dark long enough to believe she was unseen. She was not. An APSC operative rendered invisible by his own gift had been standing near her for longer than she'd realized, and when he finally spoke — *you aren't supposed to be here* — she didn't have time to answer before the world went dark around her too.

She woke strapped to a chair, one number among many, in a facility that ran through its numbers the way an assembly line runs through parts. The injections continued. Most of what happened to the people around her didn't have language attached to it yet.

Then, on an evening no different from the ones before it, she reached toward her restraints out of nothing but reflex, and the restraints moved.

She had no name for it either. For the moment, it was simply the thing that might keep her alive a little longer than the others.

### Two gifts finding each other

The subjects wanted out, and the facility's surveillance made open planning impossible, until one of them discovered he could speak inside the others' minds without speaking at all. His name, when the world eventually gave him one, would be Telepathy. His arrival in Reign's thoughts felt at first like the final proof that the trials had broken her mind completely — until she understood the voice wasn't hers, and never had been.

Together, silently, the subjects built the only plan available to them, and executed it during the chaos of another testing cycle. Telepathy coordinated. Reign tore restraints and cleared paths with a gift that had grown considerably stronger than the first frightened tug at a chair. Guards died. Doors that had never opened for prisoners opened.

Before running, they pushed deeper into the facility instead of away from it, chasing the possibility of answers in the section where the people responsible for all of it should have been sitting.

The rooms were empty. Whoever had run the trials had already left, thoroughly and in advance, the way people leave when they've known for some time that a departure was coming.

One room held something Reign didn't understand until much later: a ledger, half-burned, listing a trial numbered zero — earlier than the numbering the rest of the facility used, dated before the program's official founding, closed out not with a death or a designation but with a single line reading *subject retained by requisition*, followed by a mark that belonged to no division of APSC anyone in the room could name. No one flagged it as important. There was too much else burning.

She found no trace of Ella there. Only more questions, stacked neatly on top of the ones she'd already been carrying.

### A name and a company

She escaped with the others, and stayed close to the telepath afterward the way people who've survived the same fire tend to. When he eventually offered her a place inside APSC — its Intelligence Branch specifically, with access to records no private investigation could ever reach — she took it. Not because she trusted the company. Because she needed exactly what it had and nothing else could give her.

She chose her new name without much ceremony. "Telekinesis."

He nodded like it settled something. "Then I'll call you that."

"And you?"

"Telepathy."

She looked at him a moment longer than the answer required. "That's convenient."

He smiled in a way that could have meant several things, and told her he knew someone who might be able to help them find what she was looking for.

She heard that as hope. She did not know the warehouse had belonged to the very company she'd just agreed to serve. She did not know the experiments had been the Synthetic Ascendant program in its infancy. And she did not know that the man sitting across from her, offering her a future, was not entirely who he appeared to be — that somewhere behind his borrowed face, something else had already gone looking through her memories for an explanation of why she, alone among an entire trial, had lived.

It had not found one yet.

She wanted Ella. He wanted to know what she would eventually uncover. Neither of them understood they were walking toward the same unanswered question from two directions that had never been as opposite as they seemed — one of them searching for a woman erased from history, the other quietly guarding the machine that did the erasing.

Somewhere outside either of their understanding, Ella still existed. The Council had taken her. It had not, quite, taken every trace she left behind.

Reign had found one.

There would be others.
