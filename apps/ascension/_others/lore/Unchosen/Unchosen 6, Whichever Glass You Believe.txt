## Whichever Glass You Believe

There had been a reality where the story went exactly the way people eventually told it.

Not as metaphor. Not as a legend invented to explain one man's inability to let go. It happened, in one place, once.

The world in that reality did not end with fire, or war, or any of the things worlds usually end with. It simply lost its edges. Cities emptied without leaving graves. Homes went hollow. People vanished mid-sentence from rooms where they had been speaking a moment before, and the line between the world and its reflection stopped existing at all. There was no final battle. No one arrived to discover a hidden weakness at the last possible moment. There was simply, eventually, nothing left to save.

What remained, when it was finished, was one existence that had once been many. It walked through a world with no one left in it. It forgot the names first, then the faces, then the reasons it had ever begun at all. Even the identity of the man whose face it had chosen eventually wore away like every other name it had carried.

One memory did not wear away.

Aya.

It did not know who had loved her. It did not know what love was. It only knew that her name had outlasted everything else that had ever belonged to it, and it never learned why.

Something far outside that dead reality could still see it — a presence built to witness every branch a life could take, the ones that continued and the ones that ended. That is the only reason the story survived at all. Not because anyone inside that world remained to tell it. Because someone, somewhere, had simply seen what happened, and had not looked away.

In every other reality, the mirrors stayed mirrors. Nothing waited behind the glass in most of them. No world was ever swallowed in most of them. Yet the man at the center of the story existed in nearly all of them anyway — different centuries, different roads, always finding, always loving, always losing the same woman under a different sky.

She could love him in every one of those lives. She simply never loved him the way he needed to be loved, and that single, small, unbearable difference was the only thing that never changed no matter how many times the story started over.

He was immortal. That was the particular shape of his punishment, if it was a punishment at all, and not simply the shape a very old wound takes when nothing ever arrives to close it. He could lose people forever. He could not follow them anywhere they went. There would be no final morning that settled the question for him one way or the other — only another road, another face, another version of the same impossible woman, somewhere ahead of him, waiting to be found and lost again.

Whether the witch's curse had ever been real, or whether a frightened boy in a wet field had simply believed a stranger's cruelty long enough to make it true, no one left to ask could say. Not even the thing that had once worn his face all the way down to nothing.

Perhaps hope had never actually left him. Perhaps he had simply spent centuries teaching himself not to recognize it when it arrived.

Or perhaps some things, once said aloud by the right voice at the right moment, simply become true, and stay true, for as long as anyone is left to remember they were ever said at all.

---

She kept, until the day she died on some other road in some other reality, a small carved scrap of wood with no visible meaning to anyone who did not already know the story behind it — a gift from a man who had once loved her more than he had ever managed to say, given without expectation, kept without obligation, carried in a pocket long after his face had become difficult to remember clearly.

In an empty room, in a reality with no one left in it to notice, one mirror remained standing among thousands that had gone dark. Its surface stayed smooth. Its frame stayed whole. And when the last light in that reality finally failed, it went on holding, for no one, an image of a face that had not stood in front of it in a very long time — waiting, the way glass waits, for something to come and be seen.
