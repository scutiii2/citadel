## A Hunger Older Than the Witch

There is no good word for what waited behind the mirrors. Calling it a creature suggests it once had a childhood, a first shape, a single beginning it could point back to. Calling it a prisoner suggests the door had been shut on it by someone else. Both were partly true. Neither was the whole of it.

What is certain is this: long before it had ever taken a face, or a name, or learned the particular cruelty of choosing which parts of a stolen life to keep, it had a hunger it could not explain to itself. Not hunger for food, or for warmth, or for anything with a simple word attached. A hunger shaped like absence — like a hand reaching for a second hand that used to fit against it and finding, every time, only air.

It did not know why. It had stopped asking a long time before he arrived.

### What It Found

He walked in already carrying the thing it needed most: a memory he refused to let go of, wrapped so tightly around his sense of himself that the two had become the same object. A fire. A woman's hands. A dog breathing evenly in the dark between them.

It had taken others before him this way — pulled them through broken glass, studied what they carried, kept what was useful, discarded what wasn't. Most people, once inside, tried immediately to leave. He did not. He stood in the reflected dark and looked for a door that led backward, to a version of his own story where a woman had once said yes instead of the truth, and in looking for that door instead of an exit, he showed the thing studying him exactly where he was weakest.

It began there. Small things first. It borrowed the sound of his voice before it borrowed anything else, testing the shape of it the way a hand tests a glove before deciding to wear it.

He did not notice, not at first, that something nearby had started answering him in his own tone half a beat before he spoke.

### The Slow Theft

Time did not move the same way behind the glass. He could not have said, afterward, whether it took him days or decades to stop being entirely himself. He remembered fighting it, early — remembered standing very still and reciting, deliberately, everything he still knew to be true. His name, before Ghost. The names of people he had trained. The particular ache of a fire he had once sat beside, uninvited into someone else's heart.

It let him recite all of it. It listened the way a thief listens to a man describe exactly where he keeps his valuables.

Then, patiently, it began replacing pieces of him with copies so faithful that he mistook them for the originals. A memory of Aya's laugh, borrowed and returned slightly wrong, so gently wrong that he never noticed the seam. A memory of Maze's betrayal, sharpened at the edges until it fit more easily into the shape of every other betrayal he'd ever survived, until all of them started to feel like the same wound wearing different clothes. Somewhere in that blurring, his history stopped being a sequence of things that had happened to him and became, instead, raw material — indistinguishable from every other stolen history already layered inside the thing that was doing the stealing.

He understood, near the end of whatever could still be called his own understanding, that this was not punishment. Punishment would have required it to know, or care, what he had done to deserve this. It did not hate him. It did not love him either. It simply needed what he had, the way water needs a lower place to run toward, without malice, without a reason beyond the shape of the ground.

### What Remained

Near the very last of it, in the narrow space where he was still, technically, a single mind rather than a layer inside a larger one, something surfaced in him that had nothing to do with Aya, nothing to do with Ghost, nothing to do with any name he had ever answered to.

An image, uninvited: something sealed shut, a lock he had never chosen and could not have described the shape of. A sense — not a memory, nothing so orderly as a memory — of having once been more than one thing, in more places than one, and of all of those places closing into a single point at exactly the same instant, the way a held breath finally has to leave the body all at once.

He did not know where the image came from. He had never seen a lock like that in his life. It did not belong to him, and yet it did not feel foreign either — it felt older than anything that did belong to him, older than the witch, older than the field she had found him standing in.

He reached for a name to put to it, some instinct in him certain that naming a thing was the only way to hold onto it, and found nothing there. Not Aya. Not Ghost. Not the boy from the field before either name had ever touched him. Something else entirely, something that felt, absurdly, like it should have had a sibling — like there should have been a second version of this same ache standing somewhere just out of reach, having survived what he apparently had not.

The thought slipped away before he could close his hand around it. He never got it back.

### The Last of Him

By the time the process finished, there was very little left that could be called only his. His face remained, because it had been the freshest thing taken, and the freshest things always sat closest to the surface. His voice remained, in fragments, folded somewhere beneath thousands of other voices that had once belonged to thousands of other people who had also, at some point, stood exactly where he was standing and refused to let go of exactly one thing.

Only one memory stayed whole, untouched, uncopied, sitting apart from everything else the way a single stone sits apart from a riverbed that has worn every other stone smooth. A fire. A woman mending a tear in her sleeve. A dog breathing evenly in the space between them.

It did not know, by the end, whose memory that was. It only knew that it could not stop returning to it, the way a tongue returns to a broken tooth — not out of love, if it had ever understood what love was, but because that single unworn memory was the last edge left in a place that had otherwise gone entirely smooth.

Somewhere far behind that memory, sealed and locked and utterly forgotten by the thing that now carried his face, was a name.

Aya.

It did not remember loving her.

It only knew that it did.
